# How to open and play the project (no coding needed)

## 1. Get Godot 4.3
- The project is built for **Godot 4.3 (stable), the standard build** — not the ".NET" build, and not 4.4 or later.
- Download it from the Godot archive: https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.3-stable/
  - Windows: "Standard — 64-bit". You get a zip; unzip it anywhere. The program is `Godot_v4.3-stable_win64.exe`.
  - macOS: "Standard — Universal". Drag `Godot.app` into Applications. The first time, right-click it and choose Open.
  - Linux: "Standard — x86_64". Unzip; the program is `Godot_v4.3-stable_linux.x86_64`.
- Godot needs no installer and changes nothing else on your computer.
- (Claude Code's cloud machine has its own copy at `/root/bin/godot`, version 4.3.stable.official 77dcf97d8. That machine has no screen, so to play you need Godot on your own computer.)

## 2. Get the project
- On GitHub, open `pirateslounge420/animated-succotash` and switch the branch menu to `claude/lowpoly-exploration-prototype-i1z8g3`.
- Click **Code → Download ZIP**, and unzip it somewhere easy to find, such as Documents.
- (If you already use Git: `git clone` the repository and check out that branch.)

## 3. Open it in Godot
1. Start Godot. The **Project Manager** window opens.
2. Click **Import**, go into the unzipped folder, pick the file **`project.godot`**, and click **Import & Edit** (on some versions, **Open**).
3. The first time, Godot imports everything. Wait for the progress bar to finish (a minute or two).
   - The game no longer needs this import to look right (design §CG). The world's textures, the clouds, the species tiles and the HUD font are read straight from their files on disk, so the plants and the ground look the same whether or not the import has run or finished. Where an import is missing, the Output panel shows one warning naming the file. F3 in the game shows where the world's textures came from ("World textures: 9 from disk"). An exported build will need the raw PNGs included (see PROGRESS, 2 Oct §CG).
4. You're now in the editor. You don't need to touch anything in it.

## 4. Play
- Press **F5**, or click the **▶ Play** button at the top right. That runs the main scene, `scenes/main.tscn`.
- A game window opens. For a few seconds the planet is generated: the full one, 400 km around (a hundredth of Earth's, design §CR; `data/world_scale.json` → `planet.circumference_m`), with mountains and seas at a tenth of their height and everything you can stand next to at its real size. A brand-new world also takes a few seconds more for its sites pass (which ruins and nests keep a hearth, §CU; kept in the world's save after that). Then you wake by the campfire at the first camp.
- **The size of the world** (§CR): the ground curves away fast. From eye height the horizon is about 400 m on flat ground; a 30 m tower shows over it from 2.4 km, a 100 m hill from 4 km, and the view from a great summit reaches about 11 km. A handful of great ranges (4–7 a world, summits 500–885 m, above the low clouds) have flanks averaging about 22°, sheer faces of 60° and more on about one seventh of them (you can't climb those), and a way up to every summit that never goes steeper than 35°. **Slopes slow you as they slow a walker** (Tobler's pace, `world_scale.json` → `pace`): up a 10° slope you go at about half your flat speed, 20° about a quarter, 30° about an eighth, walking or sprinting; downhill is never faster than the flat, and ground steeper than 45° can't be walked up at all. A great summit takes 2–5 game hours from its foot.
- `data/dev.json` already sets this up:
  - `dev_mode: true` turns the F2–F9 dev keys on.
  - `postage_stamp: false` keeps play on the full planet. The "postage stamp" (a small test planet 40 km around with every kind of biome) is only for the checks and renders: they ask for it themselves with `STAMP=1` in the environment (for example `STAMP=1 godot --headless --path . --script tools/camp_check.gd`). Setting it to `true` here would make play use the stamp too; leave it off.
  - `seed: 42` and `spawn_choice: -1` are the dev tools' pins (the renders and checks set them themselves; -1 means "no pinned camp"). In play they apply only with `pin_in_play: true` (it is `false`) or `DEV_PIN=1` in the environment (design §CB). Otherwise **every new world rolls its own seed and its own first camp**, and booting continues the last world played (`user://worlds/last.json` points at it; old worlds stay by seed, nothing is deleted). The seed is the world's name: the log's first line reads "World 7731 — day 1". Every new world opens on **Day 1** at dawn (design §CY.1); the day goes up at midnight where you stand, and the HUD's time, the clock face and the log's stamps all read the same clock (design §CG). For another world: **Settings (O) > World > New world** (it asks once; this world stays saved), or **F12** twice in dev mode.
  - `day_length_min: 144` is the real cycle: 144 minutes for a full day and night (6 real minutes an in-game hour; at the equator on an equinox: day 07:00–17:00, dusk 17:00–20:00, night 20:00–04:00, dawn 04:00–07:00). You wake one real minute into dawn, the world still blue and the fire the one warm thing (design §CY.1; `data/roads.json` `opening_road.dawn_start`). The road to the first camp at a ruin is picked to be about 40 minutes of walking at the slope pace, shortened where the day there is too short to leave 20 minutes before dusk. Lower it (say 20) to see the cycle go by faster.
- To stop, press **Esc** to free the mouse and close the game window. Or, back in the editor, press the **■ Stop** button.
- **Where plants grow** (design §CA, `data/habitat.json`): a species grows only in a biome whose file lists it (or, for a `data/plants` catalogue entry, in the biomes its own `biomes` list names); climate, soil and the realm still apply inside. **Plants live in communities** (§CS, `habitat.json` → `communities`): each biome file's associations are its communities, and each land of the planet (a `RealmMap` province) is dealt one community per biome by niche (and by the land's own part of Earth where it fits); no community lives in two lands, and a land left without one takes its neighbour's. A stretch grows only its community's dominant, companion, ground and catalogue plants, each where its own climate, soil and needs fit. A biome with no associations yet (estuary, freshwater, ice sheet) and a catalogue species no association lists yet keep the placement they had. `COMMUNITIES=0` in the environment turns it off, to compare. `python3 tools/plant_schema_check.py --strict` requires the lists and prints each biome's species count and the thin spots; `python3 tools/biome_species_check.py SAVANNA` lists what the gate allows there.

## 5. Keys (keyboard and mouse)
The game plays in the **ambient profile** (`data/movement.json` `profile`, design 30 Sept §AT–§BA): a slow first-person walk. Space jumps, right click interacts and climbs, and the wall jump, cling, bounce, swing, redirect, roll and super meter are off. You wake with nothing; a bundle of torches lies by every camp fire (right click takes one, Q brings it to hand). A lit torch's head is the stick's own burnt end, smouldering (Mike, 3 Oct): six-sided and as thick as the stick, black char with hot cracks and a glowing broken tip, a few sparks, its light breathing slowly, hotter when you sprint or the wind blows, dimmer and redder as it gutters. **The swing passes the flame** (design 2 Oct §CN): left click with the torch in hand swings it, and at the end of the swing the flame passes to whatever it touches, either way: an unlit torch swung through a lit fire or a planted torch catches; a lit torch lights a laid cold fire, embers or a planted torch gone out. It never lights the ground, grass, camps, folk or animals, and does nothing to an animal. Right click the ground with a lit torch to plant it, right click it to take it back. Fires burn down: right click fuel lying about (logs, branches, brush, dung, reeds, whatever the biome offers) to gather it, right click the fire with it to feed it. Embers come back with dry fuel. **A fire that is fully out needs kindling** (§CN): right click it with kindling to lay it, with at least one piece of fuel on it, then swing a lit torch through it; the flame takes in a moment. Kindling is whatever is fine and dry where you are: right click the ground at your feet for dead twigs, leaves, needles or dry grass (crouch first if you hold a lit torch, or the right click plants it), or right click the right plant (a birch for its bark). The pouch keeps what you carry dry (§CO): only kindling gathered while it rains, outside a roof, starts damp, and it dries in the pouch in six game hours. Damp, only birch bark, fatwood and Douglas-fir pitchwood will catch; the rest smoke. You can gather in any weather. Kindling with no fuel flares and goes out. Every fire breathes (§CZ): its bed of coals pulses slowly in patches, flares when the wind gusts or a piece is laid on, and throws sparks and pale ash up at random, a burst with every pop; the light on the ground pulses with the coals. At embers the bed is the whole fire. **Every fire smokes, by its flame's size** (§CV; Mike, 3 Oct): a column of pale blue-grey smoke over every campfire (a camp's, a rekindled old hearth, a nest's, a fire you lay), its size by the fire (full, half when low, a thin wisp at embers, nothing out); the small fires send up their own, sized by the flame (smoke.json hearth.by_flame): a tomb lamp or the fat lamp a thread about a metre tall, the torch's burnt end (in your hand or planted) a faint wisp under a metre that trails behind you as you walk, the pipe's brand a wisp while it lights the bowl. **The sun flares** when you look at it (§DB; `look.json lens_flare`): a soft core and halo at the sun and two faint rings toward the middle of the frame, cool white and cyan (warm only near sunset), drawn into the picture and dithered with it; gone behind anything (a hill, a trunk, leaves, a wall), under a cloud, underground, and never the moon. Every column leans with the wind, lies flat over the ground on a still dawn and is beaten down by rain; at night only its fire-lit foot shows. The living camps up to 3.2 km away show their columns too, so you can read the land from a ridge. A barrow delve's first room and its heart each breathe through a stone vent on the mound, smoking when that hearth is lit and glowing faintly at night; a cold vent may hold a flock of swifts that hunt over the ruin by day, wheel and pour down it at dusk and out at dawn, and leave the moment the hearth below is lit. Cave mouths and grottos carry soot over the mouth. A wildfire raises a wide dark plume for its first hours. **Folk sit in the fire circle** (§CY.2–CY.4): every camp's folk, and the elder and the hunter at the opening camp, sit round the fire on seats the place supplies (logs, stumps, roots, rocks, flat stones, driftwood, hummocks, mats; fallen blocks at a ruin camp; the wood the stand's own, the stone the place's), with a spare seat; they watch the fire, warm their hands, poke it (the fire flares and throws sparks), feed it when it is low, eat, mend, doze, and one adult at a time packs a pipe, lights it with a brand from the fire and smokes, little square puffs drifting off (what's in it is a mystery herb, Mike's call; it is never named). Come within 7 m and the nearest hoods turn to you, and back to the fire when you go. Right click a camp's fire to make it your hearth. **When you die** (§DE) your body stays where you fell with everything on it, and folk find you out cold: they carry you to your hearth if it still burns with folk at it (not gone dark, overrun or abandoned), otherwise to the nearest lit fire with folk at it from where you fell; you have no hearth until you make one. One to three game days pass for real (the clock, moon, season and weather move on, every camp lives those days, fires burn down, the torch on your body burns out), and you come to by the fire at whatever hour that is, empty-handed. The log says what took you ("Struck down by a wolf") and then "Folk found you out cold and carried you to their fire. Two days have passed." **Shafts of sunlight** (§DC) show only when the air would really show them: direct sun on the place (not overcast, no cloud shadow over you), something in the air (the land's fog, the damp after rain or at dawn, the mist, a hearth's smoke, the dust in a ruin's hall) and a broken roof. That means pale cool bars slanting down through gaps in the crowns, sunbeams with dust in them in a ruin's hall, and long rays across the open sky when the cloud is broken. They are brightest looking toward the sun. **Butterflies by day** (§DC): two to six small flapping specks circle the herbs and shrubs near you in warm sun (14–40 °C, never under closed crowns, never on snow). When the wind reaches 5.5 m/s they settle in the grass, and they are gone at dusk. Their colours go by the land (whites, yellows and blues; the odd orange fleck). They don't glow. **The crag fortress** (§DO), rare, at most four a world, in high, cold, dry country: limewashed tiers of buildings climbing a rock rise 40–100 m high, a red-brown band under every roofline, a gilt finial on the top chapel, and one long stair up its sunward face. Its lesser buildings stand round a plaza at the foot, where a camp may live. Set `profile` to `shinobi` for the ninja game (the extra keys are in the "Shinobi profile only" table below). **The wind you can see** (§DA, Wind I; `data/wind.json`): the weather's wind with gusts that travel. A gust is a patch of stronger air (up to 1.6 times the mean, lulls down to 0.45, turning the direction up to 10°) carried downwind at the wind's own speed, so you see it come: a paler, bowed patch runs over the grass toward you, then the trees round you toss. Every plant reads the same field at its own spot, so neighbours move together. Under trees everything below the crowns is sheltered by how much sky that spot sees (under a closed roof the understory gets 15 % of the wind) while the crowns take the full wind. What moves follows the real Beaufort signs (`wind.json crowns`): grass and herbs bow from 1.6 m/s (nearly flat by 13.9), leaves stir from 1.6, twigs from 3.4, small branches from 5.5, small trees sway from 8, big branches from 10.8, whole trees from 13.9; each trunk sways at its own period by its height. Aspens, poplars and the sacred fig tremble in any air (`wind.json flutter`). The shadow-dapple under trees shifts with the same gusts. **Wind II:** where leaves lie (the real litter, never conjured), a gust past 5.5 m/s at the ground (after the shelter of any crowns over it) lifts them: each is its tree's own leaf in its season's colour, tumbling end over end, skipping downwind and lying down again after 2–6 s, at most 40 at once round you. Conifer needles need 9 m/s. Sand streams over dunes, beaches and hot desert from 6.5 m/s, spindrift over snow from 5, dust off dry bare ground and roads in dry country from 7, as low scrolling streaks. Under a closed wood the gusts rarely reach the ground, so this happens on roads, clearings and edges. The bed's wind loop swells with the gust at you. The crowns round you (within 40 m) are sound sources when the gust there passes 1.6 m/s, at most four at once, each from its own tree: needles hush, broad leaves rustle, palm fronds clatter, dry autumn leaves rattle. Your cloak and every cloaked figure's take the gust where they stand, sheltered by the crowns over them. The fire's specks and the torch's sparks drift with the gust at the fire, and the coals flare as a gust reaches them. **Wind III:** water is a mirror under 1 m/s; past 2.5 m/s a gust's patch roughens as it slides over (its glints break up and the blue deepens a little); from 8 m/s a few pale pixel crests on lakes and the sea, never foam sheets. On part-cloudy days (§CX's cover between 0.15 and 0.85) the clouds' shadows sail over the land at 1.8 times the ground wind, patches 200–800 m across with hard edges: in one, the lit side goes to shade (navy or olive, as shade always is) and the sun's shadows stay where they are. Clear days have none and overcast is all shade already. A hearth under trees sends its smoke straight up through the crowns and leans only above them, the lean following the gusts at each height, and a gust at the fire tears the low column into rags.

**Old hearths** (Mike, 2 Oct): wherever people once kept a fire and nobody does now (every ruin with no camp, every nest holding an old camp's remains), the hearth is still there: a ring of stones round cold ash and a couple of charred branches (they count as its fuel). Below, whatever holds it waits in the dark rooms at any hour. Each room has a cold fire-holder (the first room's old hearth; the heart's ring of ash): laid and lit, each holds its room, and nothing comes within its light while it burns (three pieces of fuel at most). The picture: the game draws at a fixed 480 lines (854×480) and blows that up to your window with square, unsmoothed pixels, text and all, so a bigger window only means bigger pixels. With integer scaling on (the default) it uses a whole multiple when the window holds at least two (a 1080p screen shows it at exactly 2×, with thin black bars round it); turn it off in settings to fill the screen at 2.25×. 720 lines is the most the settings allow.

Health: the thin blue bar at the bottom left, with its number. It doesn't come back on its own; stand or sit still by a lit campfire to heal. Nothing hostile can hurt you by a lit fire. Falls hurt (more the farther you fall), and crashing into a trunk or wall at high speed hurts, and can kill.

Dying: your body stays where you fell, with everything you carried and wore (no marker; birds start circling over it after a while). You wake by the nearest campfire, carried there by the folk who found you, with nothing. Go back and right click your body to take it all back.

Carrying: ten things at most. Past six you're slower, climb slower and make more noise.

Climbing: walk up to a tree and right click it. W and S go up and down the trunk, and A and D go around it. At a fork, look out along a limb and push W to go onto it. Push toward another limb to reach across.

## 6. If something goes wrong
- **"This project was made with a different version":** you opened it with a Godot other than 4.3. Use 4.3 (step 1).
- **A black screen for more than about 20 seconds:** the planet is still generating, or your graphics card doesn't support the Forward+ renderer. Try updating your graphics drivers.
- **An F-key does nothing:** click inside the game window first. When the editor window has focus, F-keys go to the editor instead (there, F5 runs the game and F8 stops it).
