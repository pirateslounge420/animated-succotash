# How to open and play the project (no coding needed)

## 1. Get Godot 4.3
- The project is built for **Godot 4.3 (stable), the standard build** — not the ".NET" build, and not 4.4 or later.
- Download it from the Godot archive: https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.3-stable/
  - Windows: "Standard — 64-bit". You get a zip; unzip it anywhere. The program is `Godot_v4.3-stable_win64.exe`.
  - macOS: "Standard — Universal". Drag `Godot.app` into Applications. The first time, right-click it and choose Open.
  - Linux: "Standard — x86_64". Unzip; the program is `Godot_v4.3-stable_linux.x86_64`.
- Godot needs no installer and changes nothing else on your computer.
- (Claude Code's cloud machine has its own copy at `/root/bin/godot`, version 4.3.stable.official 77dcf97d8. That machine has no screen, so to play you need Godot on your own computer.)

## 2. Get the project
- On GitHub, open `pirateslounge420/animated-succotash` and switch the branch menu to `claude/lowpoly-exploration-prototype-i1z8g3`.
- Click **Code → Download ZIP**, and unzip it somewhere easy to find, such as Documents.
- (If you already use Git: `git clone` the repository and check out that branch.)

## 3. Open it in Godot
1. Start Godot. The **Project Manager** window opens.
2. Click **Import**, go into the unzipped folder, pick the file **`project.godot`**, and click **Import & Edit** (on some versions, **Open**).
3. The first time, Godot imports everything. Wait for the progress bar to finish (a minute or two).
   - The game no longer needs this import to look right (design §CG). The world's textures, the clouds, the species tiles and the HUD font are read straight from their files on disk, so the plants and the ground look the same whether or not the import has run or finished. Where an import is missing, the Output panel shows one warning naming the file. F3 in the game shows where the world's textures came from ("World textures: 9 from disk"). An exported build will need the raw PNGs included (see PROGRESS, 2 Oct §CG).
4. You're now in the editor. You don't need to touch anything in it.

## 4. Play
- Press **F5**, or click the **▶ Play** button at the top right. That runs the main scene, `scenes/main.tscn`.
- A game window opens. For a few seconds the planet is generated: the full one, 400 km around (a hundredth of Earth's, design §CR; `data/world_scale.json` → `planet.circumference_m`), with mountains and seas at a tenth of their height and everything you can stand next to at its real size. A brand-new world also takes a few seconds more for its sites pass (which ruins and nests keep a hearth, §CU; kept in the world's save after that). Then you wake by the campfire at the first camp.
- **The size of the world** (§CR): the ground curves away fast. From eye height the horizon is about 400 m on flat ground; a 30 m tower shows over it from 2.4 km, a 100 m hill from 4 km, and the view from a great summit reaches about 11 km. A handful of great ranges (4–7 a world, summits 500–885 m, above the low clouds) have flanks averaging about 22°, sheer faces of 60° and more on about one seventh of them (you can't climb those), and a way up to every summit that never goes steeper than 35°. **Slopes slow you as they slow a walker** (Tobler's pace, `world_scale.json` → `pace`): up a 10° slope you go at about half your flat speed, 20° about a quarter, 30° about an eighth, walking or sprinting; downhill is never faster than the flat, and ground steeper than 45° can't be walked up at all. A great summit takes 2–5 game hours from its foot.
- `data/dev.json` already sets this up:
  - `dev_mode: true` turns the F2–F9 dev keys on.
  - `postage_stamp: false` keeps play on the full planet. The "postage stamp" (a small test planet 40 km around with every kind of biome) is only for the checks and renders: they ask for it themselves with `STAMP=1` in the environment (for example `STAMP=1 godot --headless --path . --script tools/camp_check.gd`). Setting it to `true` here would make play use the stamp too; leave it off.
  - `seed: 42` and `spawn_choice: -1` are the dev tools' pins (the renders and checks set them themselves; -1 means "no pinned camp"). In play they apply only with `pin_in_play: true` (it is `false`) or `DEV_PIN=1` in the environment (design §CB). Otherwise **every new world rolls its own seed and its own first camp**, and booting continues the last world played (`user://worlds/last.json` points at it; old worlds stay by seed, nothing is deleted). The seed is the world's name: the log's first line reads "World 7731 — day 1". Every new world opens on **Day 1** at dawn (design §CY.1); the day goes up at midnight where you stand, and the HUD's time, the clock face and the log's stamps all read the same clock (design §CG). For another world: **Settings (O) > World > New world** (it asks once; this world stays saved), or **F12** twice in dev mode.
  - `day_length_min: 144` is the real cycle: 144 minutes for a full day and night (6 real minutes an in-game hour; at the equator on an equinox: day 07:00–17:00, dusk 17:00–20:00, night 20:00–04:00, dawn 04:00–07:00). You wake in the blue before sunrise, once the sun is 3° below the horizon (`roads.json → opening_road.dawn_start.spawn.sun_deg`; §EB.1), the fire the one warm thing (design §CY.1; `data/roads.json` `opening_road.dawn_start`). The road to the first camp at a ruin is picked to be about 40 minutes of walking at the slope pace, shortened where the day there is too short to leave 20 minutes before dusk. Lower it (say 20) to see the cycle go by faster.
- To stop, press **Esc** to free the mouse and close the game window. Or, back in the editor, press the **■ Stop** button.
- **Where plants grow** (design §CA, `data/habitat.json`): a species grows only in a biome whose file lists it (or, for a `data/plants` catalogue entry, in the biomes its own `biomes` list names); climate, soil and the realm still apply inside. **Plants live in communities** (§CS, `habitat.json` → `communities`): each biome file's associations are its communities, and each land of the planet (a `RealmMap` province) is dealt one community per biome by niche (and by the land's own part of Earth where it fits); no community lives in two lands, and a land left without one takes its neighbour's. A stretch grows only its community's dominant, companion, ground and catalogue plants, each where its own climate, soil and needs fit. A biome with no associations yet (estuary, freshwater, ice sheet) and a catalogue species no association lists yet keep the placement they had. `COMMUNITIES=0` in the environment turns it off, to compare. `python3 tools/plant_schema_check.py --strict` requires the lists and prints each biome's species count and the thin spots; `python3 tools/biome_species_check.py SAVANNA` lists what the gate allows there.
- **The workshop** (§EL): a people's camp at the storage rung (not the opening camp, which has none) has one workshop hut 6–10 m from its fire. It is built in the people's own form and colours, with its open side to the fire. Inside are a soft bench (a hide mat) and a hard bench (a stone slab), each with the people's pieces. Beside the door are a porch seat and the people's porch sign, and once the camp has the pottery trade (§EI) a kiln stands downwind. The hearth's pot, rack and lamp stand round the fire circle, and the lamp is lit only after dusk. By day (7–17 local) the keeper and the children stay at the fire. The maker works at a bench most of the day, and everyone else does one stretch at a bench and spends the rest out gathering, out of sight. At dusk everyone walks back to the circle. Walk into reach of a bench carrying reeds or grass (they go to the soft bench) or a branch or log (the hard bench), and E lays it there. Tunables are in `camps.json → sim.workshop`, the props in each `data/peoples/*.json` `huts` block. `STAMP=1 SEED=7731 godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 --script tools/workshop_check.gd` checks it. `SITES=workshop QUICK=1` in the walkabout shows a workshop at the opening camp's site from the road at midday (a harness frame: in play the opening camp has none).
- **What a camp needs, and its trades** (§EI): every living camp makes its water trips, two a day for each ten folk. Near the player you see a folk walk to the nearest fresh water with a pot on the head and back, and after the first trip the water pot stands by the hearth. Every 10 game days a folk carries a bundle of the shelter's own material in, and a patch goes on the shelter's front (the newest six show). From the storage rung a camp takes up trades in the real order: cordage and baskets, pottery, leather and hide, woodwork, textiles, lighting, stone. Each needs the right land within 300 m (fibre or bark plants, clay soil or a river bank, trees, fine stone, oil or resin plants), its earlier trades, and for pottery, textiles and lighting a maker. A camp shows at most three: cordage first, then its maker's trade (with the trades that trade needs), then the rest in order. Each trade puts its own pieces on its bench, and the maker sits at that trade's bench (the potter at the kiln, which smokes while they work). Tunables are in `camps.json → sim.needs` and `sim.trades` (`gates` is how the land is read). `STAMP=1 SEED=7731 godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 --script tools/trades_check.gd` checks it. `SITES=pottery QUICK=1` in the walkabout shows the opening river camp at the specialist rung.
- **No metal** (§EH): nothing in the world is metal; crafts stop at clay, bone, stone, wood and fibre, and a camp can grow by its own births all the way to 50 folk, food allowing (`camps.json → sim.population.village_cap`, `births_to_village_cap`). `STAMP=1 SEED=42 godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 --script tools/no_metal_check.gd` (and `SEED=7731`) checks the peoples, the camps, techniques, village specialties, the cap and the scripts.
- **Villages** (design §EE, §EF, §EG; `data/villages.json`). One kind is built so far: the **stream-gutter town**, an abandoned stone-and-timber village on a gentle slope above the inside of a stream's bend, with a ridge behind it. A world has up to 12 of them, at least 6 km apart (1.5 km on the dev stamp). Each one is grown from its hearth square:
  - lanes run where people would walk, to the spring above, the landing on the stream and the fields either side;
  - squares sit where lanes meet;
  - houses are packed along the lanes in terraces of five and three, looser toward the edge;
  - each village has three quarters, a dry-stone wall round its edge, and a dry stone gutter down the main street;
  - one stone tower can be seen from most of the lanes;
  - one deliberate empty space: an empty square, a still pool or an unplanted bank.

  Houses stand on their slope as it is: a stone plinth, stepped footings with a dry-stone apron, or a timber floor on posts of different lengths, with steps up to raised doors. Everything is one stone, one timber and one roof material, chosen from the rock and climate. It is all mossy, ivied and a little crooked. **Every house has its own cold hearth** under its chimney, every square but the empty one has a hearth, and some views end on a cold hearth ringed with stones. They light like any old hearth: lay kindling with right click, then swing a lit torch through it (§CN). When a house's hearth burns, its windows glow and smoke leaves by its chimney. As more hearths burn, the village warms at night (§EE.1, `data/look.json → village_warmth`), one house at a time; the wild between villages stays blue. While a square's hearth is cold, brambles choke its walls and there's nowhere to sit with your back to a wall; light it and the brambles burn back and the benches are there. The log notes your first sight of a village, its first lit window, each square cleared and every hearth burning. Once you relight a village hearth it stays lit: the folk it draws back tend it (Mike, 5 Oct), so a village you have warmed stays warm.

## 5. Keys (keyboard and mouse)
The game plays in the **ambient profile** (`data/movement.json` `profile`, design 30 Sept §AT–§BA): a slow first-person walk. Space jumps, right click interacts and climbs, and the wall jump, cling, bounce, swing, redirect, roll and super meter are off. You wake holding an unlit torch and nothing else (design 4 Oct §ED.1; `items.json → starting_kit_ambient.in_hand`); a bundle of torches lies by every camp fire too (right click takes one, Q brings it to hand). A lit torch's head is the stick's own burnt end, smouldering (Mike, 3 Oct): six-sided and as thick as the stick, black char with hot cracks and a glowing broken tip, a few sparks, its light breathing slowly, hotter when you sprint or the wind blows, dimmer and redder as it gutters. **The swing passes the flame** (design 2 Oct §CN): left click with the torch in hand swings it, and at the end of the swing the flame passes to whatever it touches, either way: an unlit torch swung through a lit fire or a planted torch catches; a lit torch lights a laid cold fire, embers or a planted torch gone out. It never lights the ground, grass, camps, folk or animals, and does nothing to an animal. Right click the ground with a lit torch to plant it, right click it to take it back. Fires burn down: right click fuel lying about (logs, branches, brush, dung, reeds, whatever the biome offers) to gather it, right click the fire with it to feed it. Embers come back with dry fuel. **A fire that is fully out needs kindling** (§CN): right click it with kindling to lay it, with at least one piece of fuel on it, then swing a lit torch through it; the flame takes in a moment. Kindling is whatever is fine and dry where you are: right click the ground at your feet for dead twigs, leaves, needles or dry grass (crouch first if you hold a lit torch, or the right click plants it), or right click the right plant (a birch for its bark). The pouch keeps what you carry dry (§CO): only kindling gathered while it rains, outside a roof, starts damp, and it dries in the pouch in six game hours. Damp, only birch bark, fatwood and Douglas-fir pitchwood will catch; the rest smoke. You can gather in any weather. Kindling with no fuel flares and goes out. Every fire breathes (§CZ): its bed of coals pulses slowly in patches, flares when the wind gusts or a piece is laid on, and throws sparks and pale ash up at random, a burst with every pop; the light on the ground pulses with the coals. At embers the bed is the whole fire. **Every fire smokes, by its flame's size** (§CV; Mike, 3 Oct): a column of pale blue-grey smoke over every campfire (a camp's, a rekindled old hearth, a nest's, a fire you lay), its size by the fire (full, half when low, a thin wisp at embers, nothing out); the small fires send up their own, sized by the flame (smoke.json hearth.by_flame): a tomb lamp or the fat lamp a thread about a metre tall, the torch's burnt end (in your hand or planted) a faint wisp under a metre that trails behind you as you walk, the pipe's brand a wisp while it lights the bowl. **The sun flares** when you look at it (§DB; `look.json lens_flare`): a soft core and halo at the sun and two faint rings toward the middle of the frame, cool white and cyan (warm only near sunset), drawn into the picture and dithered with it; gone behind anything (a hill, a trunk, leaves, a wall), under a cloud, underground, and never the moon. Every column leans with the wind, lies flat over the ground on a still dawn and is beaten down by rain; at night only its fire-lit foot shows. The living camps up to 3.2 km away show their columns too, so you can read the land from a ridge. A barrow delve's first room and its heart each breathe through a stone vent on the mound, smoking when that hearth is lit and glowing faintly at night; a cold vent may hold a flock of swifts that hunt over the ruin by day, wheel and pour down it at dusk and out at dawn, and leave the moment the hearth below is lit. Cave mouths and grottos carry soot over the mouth. A wildfire raises a wide dark plume for its first hours. **Folk sit in the fire circle** (§CY.2–CY.4): every camp's folk, and the elder and the hunter at the opening camp, sit round the fire on seats the place supplies (logs, stumps, roots, rocks, flat stones, driftwood, hummocks, mats; fallen blocks at a ruin camp; the wood the stand's own, the stone the place's), with a spare seat; they watch the fire, warm their hands, poke it (the fire flares and throws sparks), feed it when it is low, eat, mend, doze, and one adult at a time packs a pipe, lights it with a brand from the fire and smokes, little square puffs drifting off (what's in it is a mystery herb, Mike's call; it is never named). Come within 7 m and the nearest hoods turn to you, and back to the fire when you go. Right click a camp's fire to make it your hearth. **Three hits, no bar** (§EA amended by §EC, `data/harm.json`; the ambient game only, the ninja game keeps its health meter): there is no health meter. A creature's hit takes no health but counts one, and every hit is unmistakable: a dark-navy flash at the frame's edges (never red), a small camera kick, a thud and a breath, and a hitstop of a few frames. For `invuln_s` (0.6 s) after a hit, no other lands, so a pack can't take you in one blink. One hit heals every `recover.step_s` (5 s), and any new hit resets that timer; it ticks even while you're chased. After one hit the edges darken and the colour drains toward navy (never grey), with the sound a little muffled. After two it goes deeper and a fast heartbeat (150 bpm) comes up under everything; healing back to one, the heartbeat settles first, then the dark pulls back. A third before they heal takes you: the frame closes to black, **Good night** shows big and red, holds, fades, and folk find you as below. The dark's own catch (§BA) still takes you at once. Every death in this game (a fall, the dark's catch) closes the same way, with "Good night", never "You died". Your own arrow falling back on you only glances off. Goblins are out of play for now (their data stays). **When you die** (§DE) your body stays where you fell with everything on it, and folk find you out cold: they carry you to your hearth if it still burns with folk at it (not gone dark, overrun or abandoned), otherwise to the nearest lit fire with folk at it from where you fell; you have no hearth until you make one. One to three game days pass for real (the clock, moon, season and weather move on, every camp lives those days, fires burn down, the torch on your body burns out), and you come to by the fire at whatever hour that is, empty-handed. The log says what took you ("Struck down by a wolf") and then "Folk found you out cold and carried you to their fire. Two days have passed." **Shafts of sunlight** (§DC) show only when the air would really show them: direct sun on the place (not overcast, no cloud shadow over you), something in the air (the land's fog, the damp after rain or at dawn, the mist, a hearth's smoke, the dust in a ruin's hall) and a broken roof. That means pale cool bars slanting down through gaps in the crowns, sunbeams with dust in them in a ruin's hall, and long rays across the open sky when the cloud is broken. They are brightest looking toward the sun. **Butterflies by day** (§DC): two to six small flapping specks circle the herbs and shrubs near you in warm sun (14–40 °C, never under closed crowns, never on snow). | Right mouse held near a branch | Catch and swing on a branch, bamboo or vine; let go to fly on (green bamboo springs you out; dead, grey wood snaps) |
| Left mouse past full charge | Super meter (the gold ring round the gauge, filled by perfect wall jumps, rolls and swing releases, and by hits): keep holding past full charge until the gauge fills again in red for a super shot (triple damage, faster, farther, a red streak); a super-thrown spear kills what it hits outright and pins it. At 25 m/s a spear thrust kills anything but a mythical creature; a thrust into a trunk or wall at speed hurts you instead |
| V or F5 | Switch between first and third person |

Gamepad:
- Left stick moves, and clicking it in (held) sprints.
- A jumps, B sneaks, X interacts, Y cycles what's in hand.
- The right trigger draws and shoots.
- Start opens the inventory, Back the map.
- Shinobi profile only: B in the air drops fast (and rolls on landing), A at a wall wall-jumps, X held clings and swings, and clicking the right stick switches first and third person.

Readouts: your speed at the bottom right (mph and km/h; faint when you're slow, brighter toward 120 km/h; in the shinobi profile its glow warms as the super meter fills), and an old railway pocket watch at the top right: a steel case and crown, a white dial with the hours 1-12 in black and 13-24 in red inside them, a minute track, black hands and a red seconds hand (the minute hand goes round once a game hour, 6 real minutes; the seconds hand once a game minute). These two are the only readouts pinned in a new game; the others (the time, the moon, its mansion, the season, the biome and soil, the temperature, the weather, the wind, your elevation and position) show with H, or all the time once you pin them (Esc, then click them). "Swimming" and "Above the clouds" show whenever they apply. All the on-screen text grows with the window.

The picture: the game draws at a fixed 480 lines (854×480) and blows that up to your window with square, unsmoothed pixels, text and all, so a bigger window only means bigger pixels. With integer scaling on (the default) it uses a whole multiple when the window holds at least two (a 1080p screen shows it at exactly 2×, with thin black bars round it); turn it off in settings to fill the screen at 2.25×. 720 lines is the most the settings allow.

Health: the thin blue bar at the bottom left, with its number. It doesn't come back on its own; stand or sit still by a lit campfire to heal. Nothing hostile can hurt you by a lit fire. Falls hurt (more the farther you fall), and crashing into a trunk or wall at high speed hurts, and can kill.

Dying: your body stays where you fell, with everything you carried and wore (no marker; birds start circling over it after a while). You wake by the nearest campfire, carried there by the folk who found you, with nothing. Go back and right click your body to take it all back.

Carrying: ten things at most. Past six you're slower, climb slower and make more noise.

Climbing: walk up to a tree and right click it. W and S go up and down the trunk, and A and D go around it. At a fork, look out along a limb and push W to go onto it. Push toward another limb to reach across.

## 6. If something goes wrong
- **"This project was made with a different version":** you opened it with a Godot other than 4.3. Use 4.3 (step 1).
- **A black screen for more than about 20 seconds:** the planet is still generating, or your graphics card doesn't support the Forward+ renderer. Try updating your graphics drivers.
- **An F-key does nothing:** click inside the game window first. When the editor window has focus, F-keys go to the editor instead (there, F5 runs the game and F8 stops it).
