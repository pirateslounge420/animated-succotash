# How to open and play the project (no coding needed)

## 1. Get Godot 4.3
- The project is built for **Godot 4.3 (stable), the standard build** — not the ".NET" build, and not 4.4 or later.
- Download it from the Godot archive: https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.3-stable/
  - Windows: "Standard — 64-bit". You get a zip; unzip it anywhere. The program is `Godot_v4.3-stable_win64.exe`.
  - macOS: "Standard — Universal". Drag `Godot.app` into Applications. The first time, right-click it and choose Open.
  - Linux: "Standard — x86_64". Unzip; the program is `Godot_v4.3-stable_linux.x86_64`.
- Godot needs no installer and changes nothing else on your computer.
- (Claude Code's cloud machine has its own copy at `/root/bin/godot`, version 4.3.stable.official 77dcf97d8. That machine has no screen, so to play you need Godot on your own computer. A fresh cloud machine may start without it. In that case: unzip `Godot_v4.3-stable_linux.x86_64.zip` from the 4.3-stable GitHub release (check it against the release's `SHA512-SUMS.txt`) to `/root/bin/godot`, and run `godot --headless --path . --import` once so the scripts' class names are known. Run the import again after adding a new `class_name` script. The rendered checks under `xvfb-run` also need a software Vulkan driver: `apt-get install mesa-vulkan-drivers` (lavapipe).)

## 2. Get the project
- On GitHub, open `pirateslounge420/animated-succotash` and switch the branch menu to `claude/lowpoly-exploration-prototype-i1z8g3`.
- Click **Code → Download ZIP**, and unzip it somewhere easy to find, such as Documents.
- (If you already use Git: `git clone` the repository and check out that branch.)

## 3. Open it in Godot
1. Start Godot. The **Project Manager** window opens.
2. Click **Import**, go into the unzipped folder, pick the file **`project.godot`**, and click **Import & Edit** (on some versions, **Open**).
3. The first time, Godot imports everything. Wait for the progress bar to finish (a minute or two).
   - The game no longer needs this import to look right (design §CG). The world's textures, the clouds, the species tiles and the HUD font are read straight from their files on disk, so the plants and the ground look the same whether or not the import has run or finished. Where an import is missing, the Output panel shows one warning naming the file. F3 in the game shows where the world's textures came from ("World textures: 9 from disk"). An exported build will need the raw PNGs included (see PROGRESS, 2 Oct §CG).
4. You're now in the editor. You don't need to touch anything in it.

## 4. Play
- Press **F5**, or click the **▶ Play** button at the top right. That runs the boot scene, `scenes/boot.tscn`, which opens the game `data/game.json` names (design 6 Oct §ET.2): **Torchfire 1, the dungeon crawler** (`scenes/crawler.tscn`), by default. The open world is **Torchfire 2**, shelved but kept whole: set `"game": "torchfire2"` in `data/game.json` (or run with `GAME=torchfire2`) to play it. Everything after the Torchfire 1 block below is the open world, and every check in `tools/` that boots `scenes/main.tscn` still runs it, whatever `game.json` says.
- **Torchfire 1: the tomb** (§ET.11, the first slice; `data/crawler.json`). The screen fades up from black. You lie on a reed mat in a stone room underground, by a lit hearth. Across the fire, a little to one side, the one who found you sits on a low stone: a cloaked figure with a beast's head (§EO), now a solid 3D figure you can walk all the way round (§FH, not a flat sprite), painted in big pixels the size of the walls' own, lit orange on the side that faces the fire and navy behind, its shadow thrown over the floor and up the wall. It breathes slowly, and turns its head to watch you while you're in front of it. Beside the hearth lies a bundle of three unlit torches. Nothing on screen tells you what to do (§ET.3: the room teaches without words).
  - **On screen** (§EX.7): only a small crosshair in the middle of the view, the open world's (`hud.json → reticle`): four short light-blue arms, each on a one-pixel dark edge, drawn on the frame's own pixels so it is as chunky as the rest of the picture. It keeps its size on screen at every pixel size (arms 5 pixels at 480 lines, 3 at 270). It gives off no light, and there are still no words, prompts or meters. Settings (O) → **Crosshair dot** turns it off. It hides while the log or the settings panel is open. While you sneak, its arms close into a small, dimmer ring (§FC.1, below).
  - **Light:** right click by the bundle puts an unlit torch in your hand (another right click takes a spare). Left click swings it; swing it through the hearth and it catches. The mouse wheel puts it away or takes it out again (Q does nothing here), and a lit torch put away goes out. Every fire's light is one amber (§EX.6, `look.json → fire.light.color`): the torch lights the stone the same orange as the hearth and the sconces, only nearer and weaker; a guttering torch goes dimmer and redder, never paler. Even with your nose to the wall the stone at the torch stays amber (`crawler.json → firelight`: pale stone the fire blows out is put back on the fire's amber before the night grade). The dark past the light stays navy, and the daylight down the hearth's shaft stays blue.
  - **The pitch torch** (§EZ.2, `torch.json → pitch_head`): every torch in the tomb (in your hand, in the bundle, planted) has its top wound in four bands of fibre soaked black with pitch, six flat sides (never round), each band's edge standing out a little so the bands show as steps, and two to four drips of pitch running down the stick below. Lit, the top band thins to dark amber under a glowing coal (the burnt end's coal and its slow breath), and on top sits the campfire's flame in the hearth's amber, drawn on a grid 1.6 times coarser so it stays chunky close up, with a couple of single-pixel sparks. In your hand the flame is drawn at 0.55 of a planted torch's (`flame.view_scale`), so it sits at the bottom right, about a quarter of the frame tall (119 px of 480 lines, 57 of 270). It leans back against your motion, 6° for each m/s (about 26° walking, 34° sprinting), stretches to 1.3 times its height at a sprint, leans toward an airway's draft, is laid flat out at 50° (`max_deg`) in a strong mouth's gust, and stands up again within about a second when you stop; none of that can put the torch out. The torch's light flickers with the flame as well as breathing with the coal, and its smoke is a little sootier (darker navy, never grey). Unlit (the bundle, a torch smothered with F or put out with burn left) it is the pitch head alone; burnt out, a bare stick. The pitch is painted, not shiny: light shows its colours but never gives it a highlight, and in the dark it is dark. It draws over the hearth's pool of light on the floor, so the bundle's heads stay dark. (The near-black pitch shows as the frame's darkest navy, as every near-black does under the night grade.) Tunables in `torch.json → pitch_head`: the head's length, swell, bands and drips, the three colours, `flame.texel_scale` and `flame.view_scale`, the lean (`per_mps`, `max_deg`, `sprint_stretch`, `settle_s`) and `smoke.soot_scale`. The open world (Torchfire 2) keeps the burnt end.
  - **The tomb:** three or four doorways lead out of the hearth room. Beyond them, generated from the seed, are corridors, flights of stairs down, and rooms of the tomb kit: a crypt of stone coffins, a catacomb with bone niches, an ossuary, a collapsed room, and at the deepest point the heart, with the skeleton leaning out of its mossy coffin. Each launch is a new tomb; `SEED=123` in the environment pins one, and Settings' "New world" makes a new one.
  - **What lives in the dark** (§FE, queue 58; `data/residents.json → creatures.skeleton`): skeletons, three to six a tomb, more of them the deeper you go, none in the hearth room. They lie still in their places: in a catacomb, sitting hunched on the bottom shelf of a niche with a stone frame round it; in a crypt, kneeling in a coffin whose lid lies shoved off on the floor beside it, slumped over its end; and the heart's coffin always holds one (Mike's frame 9). They are sprites baked from a bone model (§ET.8), eight ways round and from three heights, like the rescuer.
    - **Waking:** come within 3 m of one's head (`wakes_m`), with nothing between you, and you hear bone grinding on stone as it climbs out (`rise_s`, 1.6 s). Then it comes after you, slowly (`walk_mps` 2.2; your walk is 4.3), finding its way through the doors and round the coffins. Its bare feet click on the stone. Asleep, it is only set dressing: a swing at it meets nothing.
    - **What it notices** (`notice`): you yourself within 6 m, but only along a clear line from its eyes to your head; your lit torch's flame, or the stone the torch lights round you, within 10 m, along a clear line; your steps within 6 m walking (about 17 m sprinting, under 2 m sneaking; standing still, nothing); and a swing of yours that lands on it, which is as loud as a sprint even standing still.
    - **Its strike** (`strike`, queue 57's strike, shared by every creature): within 1.6 m (`reach_m`), seeing you there, it winds up for 0.7 s (`wind_up_s`). Its jaw drops open and its claws go back, and from that first moment you hear the jaw's creak and knock (`sound`), so you can tell one is about to strike even from the side. Then the strike is committed: a 0.15 s swing (`strike_s`), and the hit lands at its end if you are still within reach with nothing solid between. Back off in time and it misses. It draws back for 1.2 s (`recover_s`) before it can wind up again.
    - **The stagger** (§FA.1, `torch.json → stagger`): swing your **lit** torch into it during its wind-up and it reels back 0.6 m (`strike.reel_m`), its strike broken, with no damage, and you hear its bones crack. It can't be staggered again for 3 s. An unlit torch does nothing, and a swing once the strike is committed is too late: the hit lands.
    - **It gives up** (`gives_up`, through queue 56's chase rules, `Pursuit`) when you are more than 16 m away, or when it hasn't seen you, your light or heard you for 8 s; crouched out of its sight behind cover counts (`hide`). Smothering your torch doesn't end its chase by itself (`torch_doused` false), but in the dark behind cover it can't see you. Then it walks back to its niche or coffin and lies down again, and it can wake again. If it senses you on the way back, it comes after you again.
    - **Fire pots burn them** (§FA.3, queue 60; `fire_hp` 3, `oil_scale`): a pot's burst within its `splash_m`, tar stuck on one, or a burning patch it stands in burns its fire hit points down. Light oil (4) burns one out at once; tar (1, then 0.5 a second while it burns on it, more in the patch) takes a few seconds. At 0 it goes up in a last flare and is gone for good, and its chase with it. One burnt while it lies asleep wakes and climbs out burning. Awake, one sees a pot's lit wick as your flame out to 25 m (`fire_pots.json → gives_away.flare_seen_m`), even with your torch smothered, and hears a burst within 40 m (`burst_heard_m`).
    - **Not yet** (queue 59): nothing keeps a skeleton out of the light. It follows you into lit rooms whether or not it has hit you, can strike you there (the snake can't), and walks home through them.
  - **Hiding** (§FC.2, `stealth.json → hide`): there is no button and no prompt. Crouch (Shift) behind a coffin with its lid on, the heart's coffin or the collapsed room's slab, and a skeleton can't see you. Keep still and quiet until it gives you up. A lit torch gives you away round cover: it sees the flame or the lit stone round you (`glow_reach_m`, 4 m). Smother it (F), or put it away with the wheel, to hide in the dark; you relight at the hearth or any holder you have lit.
  - **Hits** (§EA, §EC, with §FD's red ring, §FJ.3; Harm in the crawler since queue 49): each hit flashes dark navy in from the frame's edges and kicks the view. Hit 1 closes a thin red ring round the edge of the view and your heart starts beating; hit 2 makes the ring darker and deeper and the heart pound faster; hit 3 closes the frame to black with "Good night" (the numbers are under **Harm** below). Then you wake on the mat by the hearth, unhurt, with every light you relit still burning and your torch out in your pack, and whatever was after you gives up and goes home. You heal one hit every 5 s, **but only once nothing is after you** (§FD): a skeleton hunting you holds the healing off until it gives you up. Light doesn't heal.
  - **The walls** (§EU, `data/masonry.json`): fitted stone in real relief, the Inca way. Every wall face is cut into its own irregular many-sided stones (its own seed, so the two sides of a corridor never mirror) that fit edge to edge with hairline joints and no mortar; each stone stands a little proud with a gently bulging face, the joints sink into navy shade, and the torch catches the bulges. A few stones have settled (a little out or in, a slight turn, now and then one dropped). Which style a place gets is `by_theme`: the tomb is `fitted_small` (many tight stones), the snow ruins `megalithic` (a few huge blocks). Moss and vines grow only where the place's climate allows (`vines.json → climate`): the climate is the place's world's biome (`worlds.json`; the snow ruins are the tundra's, too cold for moss), and the tomb, which no world holds yet, uses `overgrowth.stand_in` (damp and mild). Damp walls gather moss in their joints and up their lower stones and grow thin vines from the cracks and wall tops; dry places (a desert) stay bare, with sand drifted against the wall's foot and into the corners. Underground firelit stone goes amber (§EU.6).
  - **The vents** (§EV, `smoke.json → vents`): every built-in fire has its own vent up to the surface, straight up or kinked round whatever stands over it, its mouth overhead, never a way out. A hearth's, hearth ring's or altar's vent is a shaft (0.6–1.2 m, narrower the deeper it goes), and a column of sky-light falls through it beside the fire, fading with the shaft's length (full to 3 m, none past 25 m). It follows the game clock (§FG): the crawler runs the 144-minute day, and the column brightens through the morning and dims through the afternoon with the sun's height, turns from day blue to a faint moonlit blue through dusk, and back through dawn (`smoke.json → vents.daylight`: `sky_band_deg` for the twilight, `low_sun_share` for how weak it is with the sun on the horizon). You wake at about 3 in the afternoon on the tomb's sky (the sun 43° up): the sun sets about 22 minutes into play and it is night about 31 minutes in, for 48 minutes. One clock runs everywhere (§FK.3): no latitude, no axial tilt, no seasons, so every world shares the same sunset, every day. A sconce's or brazier's vent is a narrow flue (0.15–0.3 m) that lets in no light. The tomb's one hearth is the only fire with a shaft, so the column beside it is the only daylight in the tomb (§EX.4). Every vent's mouth is stained navy-black with soot, lit or not, and a lit fire's flame leans a little toward its vent and flickers in the draft. Each vent remembers what it will become on the surface (a stack, a sooted slot) for when the tomb gets a world above it (§EV.4, §EW, not built yet).
  - **Atmosphere** (§FG, `crawler.json → ambience`; never a puzzle, never a way-marker):
    - **Glow-moss:** on damp stone, small patches of moss glow a faint cold blue-green in the dark (6 to 16 in a tomb, about ten on average, low on the walls). Its own moss texels glow, ragged at the edges, and cast a faint light on the stone and floor around them. It grows only where the stone is damp: where the moss is thickest by the place's climate (§EU.4), and more by the airways, never on dry stone, never right by a fire, never in the hearth room. Bring any flame within 3 m with nothing in between (your torch, a relit sconce) and it fades to a tenth of its glow in 0.6 s; once the flame has gone it creeps back over 6 seconds. By torchlight, up close, the patch is just moss on the stone. It is drawn in the stone, not a light, so nothing in the game counts it as light. Tunables in `ambience.glow_moss` (`color`, `energy`, `dims_near_flame_m`, `dim_to`, `dims_s`, `returns_s`, and where it grows: `damp_min`, `near_airway_m`, `airway_damp`, `per_m`, `spacing_m`, `max`, `radius_m`, `height_m`, `clear_of_fire_m`; its light: `light_m`, `light_strength`).
    - **Beetles and scarabs:** ten to sixteen small dark beetles, about one in five a rounder scarab, crawl the walls and rest now and then, more on damp walls away from the fires. When a flame's light falls on one within 3 m, it scuttles to the nearest joint between the stones and squeezes in, gone in under a second. After 8 to 22 seconds it comes out again, once that spot is dark. Tunables in `ambience.wall_life` (`count`, `scarab_share`, `size_m`, `crawl_mps`, `scatter_from_light_m`, `flee_mps`, `gone_within_s`, `hide_s`, and more).
    - Not built yet: stairs up to a window over the wilderness (with the surface ruins, §EW.7), and vines that sway as you pass (with a jungle style).
  - **Relighting** (§ET.4, §EX.4, `crawler.json → room_torches`): the hearth room's hearth is the tomb's only hearth. Every other room has cold wall torches (sconces) on its two long walls, facing each other in pairs (where doorways leave no room for that, a pair steps apart along its walls): two in a room up to 8 m long, four in a longer one (the pairs a whole number of 2 m modules apart), and four in the heart, two on each side wall flanking the dead. They keep 0.6 m clear of every doorway and corner. The rooms make room for them: a crypt's coffin rows are set so a torch sits between two coffins where that costs none (otherwise it hangs over a coffin's head, and you swing at it from the gap beside it), a catacomb leaves out the column of bone niches where a torch hangs, and bone heaps, rubble and grave goods keep clear of the floor in front of one. A room's torch gives more light than a corridor's (`light_scale`, 1.5), so two of them light a small room at least as well as its old hearth ring did. The corridors keep a cold sconce every 7 m. Between the lights it is dark: a faint navy, and by day the faint blue under the hearth's shaft. Swing a lit torch through a holder and it catches a moment later. It then stays lit for the rest of the game, lights its stretch of the tomb, and relights a dead torch you swing through it. The log (Enter) counts the lights burning again.
  - **The torch stays lit** (§ET.7 as amended by §EZ.1 and §EZ.5, `torch.json → snuff`): walking, sprinting flat out for as long as you like, turning, whipping the view round and swinging it never put it out or make it gutter. Running feeds the coal air, so it glows a little brighter at a run (`torch.json → ember.air_brighten`).
    - The airways only move it. A dark slot in a corridor wall leans the torch's flame and smoke toward it and makes its light flicker a little (`crawler.json → airways.flicker`). A marked mouth low in a room wall (a carved frame with two notches in its lintel) moans and streams dust for 1.5 s, then gusts: a torch in its line, not behind a coffin or out of the way, is whipped hard, its flame laid flat out away from the mouth, its smoke streaming flat and its light flickering, and it holds.
    - Deep water is the one thing that puts it out, and it warns first: wading toward `douse_depth_m` (0.6 m) makes the torch gutter (the coal dimmer, the flame lower and redder, flickering, a sputter you hear), and past it the torch goes out. The tombs have no water yet.
    - It still burns down: a torch lasts 50 real minutes (`torch.json → burn_min`), gutters through its last 12%, and ends as a charred stick that won't relight. The crawler's own burn time and the three-torch limit (§FJ.4) are not built yet.
  - Out means out: relight at the hearth or any holder you have lit.
  - **Smother your torch** (§FC.3, `stealth.json → douse`): F (the d-pad's down on a gamepad) puts the lit torch in your hand out on purpose, with a short hiss under your hand and one line in the log, "You smothered the torch." You keep holding it, with the burn it had left, and nothing that watches for a flame can see it any more; a hunter chasing you whose rules say so gives you up at once (`Pursuit`, its `gives_up → torch_doused`, §FD): the snake does (below). A skeleton's rules don't (its `torch_doused` is false), but smothered, your light no longer gives you away round cover (Hiding, above). It isn't water. Swing it through the hearth, any sconce or hearth ring you have relit, or a planted torch, and it catches again. F with no lit torch does nothing.
  - **A dark you can half see in** (§FC.4, `crawler.json → dark`): with no flame near you, the dark isn't blind. The walls, the floor and the doorways within about 5 m (`readable_m`) show faintly in the dark's own navy (`readable_color`), fading to nothing by about 12 m (`black_m`); past that it is black and you go by sound. It comes up over about a second as your eyes adjust (`adjust_s`). It is never on while your torch is lit, nor while a lit fire's light reaches you (the hearth, a relit sconce or ring, a planted torch, a fire pot's burning tar or burst, or a lit wick, in sight and within its light's reach; it eases off over `fade_s` as you come to one), so torchlit and firelit stone look exactly as they did. A fire round a corner doesn't count. `fill_energy` sets how bright the half-dark is (below about 1.5 nothing shows at all: the frame's own navy floor swallows it), and `falloff` how much it dims with distance before 12 m (0 is even).
  - **Sneaking** (§FC.1, `data/stealth.json → sneak`): hold Shift.
    - The view glides down into a crouch over 0.18 s and back up when you let go, never a snap. Under a low ceiling you stay down until there is room to stand.
    - The crosshair closes into a small ring at three quarters of its strength while you sneak, and opens back into the cross when you stand.
    - Your steps play at a quarter of a walking step's volume, and you make a tenth of the noise.
    - You can't walk off a drop deeper than half a metre: you stop at the lip, and pushing sideways slides you along it. Let go of Shift to step off. It doesn't hold you in the air.
    - **Tunables:** `camera_ease_s`, `reticle` (`shape`, `dim`, `ring_px`), `footstep_volume`, `ledge_guard`, `ledge_drop_m`. The open world keeps its own crouch.
  - **Two hands** (§FB, `data/hands.json`): the mouse wheel steps what your right hand holds: the torch (while you carry one) and bare hands, and later a spear once found. Your left hand holds left-hand things, the fire pots (below), which sit in their own small strip of the pack, not in the carry slots: 3 places (`left.strip_slots`), and `fire_pots.json → carry_max` pots at most. Hold Tab and scroll to step the left hand through them and back to empty; it won't change while a pot is being lit or aimed. While Tab is held a small strip shows low on the left: a square for each choice, icons only (a tar pot's plug black, a light-oil pot's pale), the one in hand marked; a quick tap of Tab shows nothing and does nothing (`tab_hold.show_after_s`, 0.15 s). The pot in your left hand shows low left in view. A trackpad's scroll steps once per stroke, a mouse wheel once per notch (`wheel_step`).
  - **Controls** (§FB): Settings (O) has two tabs at the top, Settings and Controls. The Controls page lists every action with its keys and mouse buttons, the wheel among them: moving, the hands and the screens on the left; the open world's own keys and the dev keys on the right. Click an action, then press its new key or mouse button, or turn the wheel; Esc cancels. That one input replaces the action's keys and buttons (a gamepad's stay). An input that two actions share in one game shows amber on both, with a line at the foot saying so. "Wheel drives" gives the plain wheel to your left hand instead; then Tab and the wheel drive the right, and holding Tab shows the right hand's choices low on the right. "Reset to defaults" asks once (click it again). Your choices are saved for you alone in `controls.cfg` in Godot's user folder (on a Mac, `~/Library/Application Support/Godot/app_userdata/Low-Poly Exploration/`), in force at once and at every start; delete the file to go back to the defaults.
  - **Keys:** WASD, W W (hold) sprint, Shift (hold) sneak (and hide behind cover), Space jump, mouse to look; right click take, left click swing; the wheel the right hand (torch or bare hands); F smother the torch; hold Tab and scroll the wheel to put a fire pot in your left hand or empty it, then hold left click to light it off a lit torch and aim, and let go to throw; Enter the log, O settings (and its Controls page, to rebind any of these), F11 pixel size, F2 frame time, F9 fire pots (dev). ## 5. Keys (keyboard and mouse)
The game plays in the **ambient profile** (`data/movement.json` `profile`, design 30 Sept §AT–§BA): a slow first-person walk. Space jumps, right click interacts and climbs, and the wall jump, cling, bounce, swing, redirect, roll and super meter are off. You wake holding an unlit torch and nothing else (design 4 Oct §ED.1; `items.json → starting_kit_ambient.in_hand`); a bundle of torches lies by every camp fire too (right click takes one, Q brings it to hand). A lit torch's head is the stick's own burnt end, smouldering (Mike, 3 Oct): six-sided and as thick as the stick, black char with hot cracks and a glowing broken tip, a few sparks, its light breathing slowly, hotter when you sprint or the wind blows, dimmer and redder as it gutters. **The swing passes the flame** (design 2 Oct §CN): left click with the torch in hand swings it, and at the end of the swing the flame passes to whatever it touches, either way: an unlit torch swung through a lit fire or a planted torch catches; a lit torch lights a laid cold fire, embers or a planted torch gone out. It never lights the ground, grass, camps, folk or animals, and does nothing to an animal. Right click the ground with a lit torch to plant it, right click it to take it back. Fires burn down: right click fuel lying about (logs, branches, brush, dung, reeds, whatever the biome offers) to gather it, right click the fire with it to feed it. Embers come back with dry fuel. **A fire that is fully out needs kindling** (§CN): right click it with kindling to lay it, with at least one piece of fuel on it, then swing a lit torch through it; the flame takes in a moment. Kindling is whatever is fine and dry where you are: right click the ground at your feet for dead twigs, leaves, needles or dry grass (crouch first if you hold a lit torch, or the right click plants it), or right click the right plant (a birch for its bark). The pouch keeps what you carry dry (§CO): only kindling gathered while it rains, outside a roof, starts damp, and it dries in the pouch in six game hours. Damp, only birch bark, fatwood and Douglas-fir pitchwood will catch; the rest smoke. You can gather in any weather. Kindling with no fuel flares and goes out. Every fire breathes (§CZ): its bed of coals pulses slowly in patches, flares when the wind gusts or a piece is laid on, and throws sparks and pale ash up at random, a burst with every pop; the light on the ground pulses with the coals. At embers the bed is the whole fire. **Every fire smokes, by its flame's size** (§CV; Mike, 3 Oct): a column of pale blue-grey smoke over every campfire (a camp's, a rekindled old hearth, a nest's, a fire you lay), its size by the fire (full, half when low, a thin wisp at embers, nothing out); the small fires send up their own, sized by the flame (smoke.json hearth.by_flame): a tomb lamp or the fat lamp a thread about a metre tall, the torch's burnt end (in your hand or planted) a faint wisp under a metre that trails behind you as you walk, the pipe's brand a wisp while it lights the bowl. **The sun flares** when you look at it (§DB; `look.json lens_flare`): a soft core and halo at the sun and two faint rings toward the middle of the frame, cool white and cyan (warm only near sunset), drawn into the picture and dithered with it; gone behind anything (a hill, a trunk, leaves, a wall), under a cloud, underground, and never the moon. Every column leans with the wind, lies flat over the ground on a still dawn and is beaten down by rain; at night only its fire-lit foot shows. The living camps up to 3.2 km away show their columns too, so you can read the land from a ridge. A barrow delve's first room and its heart each breathe through a stone vent on the mound, smoking when that hearth is lit and glowing faintly at night; a cold vent may hold a flock of swifts that hunt over the ruin by day, wheel and pour down it at dusk and out at dawn, and leave the moment the hearth below is lit. Cave mouths and grottos carry soot over the mouth. A wildfire raises a wide dark plume for its first hours. **Folk sit in the fire circle** (§CY.2–CY.4): every camp's folk, and the elder and the hunter at the opening camp, sit round the fire on seats the place supplies (logs, stumps, roots, rocks, flat stones, driftwood, hummocks, mats; fallen blocks at a ruin camp; the wood the stand's own, the stone the place's), with a spare seat; they watch the fire, warm their hands, poke it (the fire flares and throws sparks), feed it when it is low, eat, mend, doze, and one adult at a time packs a pipe, lights it with a brand from the fire and smokes, little square puffs drifting off (what's in it is a mystery herb, Mike's call; it is never named). Come within 7 m and the nearest hoods turn to you, and back to the fire when you go. Right click a camp's fire to make it your hearth. **Three hits, no bar** (§EA amended by §EC, `data/harm.json`; the ambient game only, the ninja game keeps its health meter): there is no health meter. A creature's hit takes no health but counts one, and every hit is unmistakable: a dark-navy flash at the frame's edges (never red), a small camera kick, a thud and a breath, and a hitstop of a few frames. For `invuln_s` (0.6 s) after a hit, no other lands, so a pack can't take you in one blink. One hit heals every `recover.step_s` (5 s), and any new hit resets that timer; it ticks even while you're chased. After one hit the edges darken and the colour drains toward navy (never grey), with the sound a little muffled. After two it goes deeper and a fast heartbeat (150 bpm) comes up under everything; healing back to one, the heartbeat settles first, then the dark pulls back. A third before they heal takes you: the frame closes to black, **Good night** shows big and red, holds, fades, and folk find you as below. The dark's own catch (§BA) still takes you at once. Every death in this game (a fall, the dark's catch) closes the same way, with "Good night", never "You died". Your own arrow falling back on you only glances off. Goblins are out of play for now (their data stays). **When you die** (§DE) your body stays where you fell with everything on it, and folk find you out cold: they carry you to your hearth if it still burns with folk at it (not gone dark, overrun or abandoned), otherwise to the nearest lit fire with folk at it from where you fell; you have no hearth until you make one. One to three game days pass for real (the clock, moon, season and weather move on, every camp lives those days, fires burn down, the torch on your body burns out), and you come to by the fire at whatever hour that is, empty-handed. The log says what took you ("Struck down by a wolf") and then "Folk found you out cold and carried you to their fire. Two days have passed." **Shafts of sunlight** (§DC) show only when the air would really show them: direct sun on the place (not overcast, no cloud shadow over you), something in the air (the land's fog, the damp after rain or at dawn, the mist, a hearth's smoke, the dust in a ruin's hall) and a broken roof. That means pale cool bars slanting down through gaps in the crowns, sunbeams with dust in them in a ruin's hall, and long rays across the open sky when the cloud is broken. They are brightest looking toward the sun. **Butterflies by day** (§DC): two to six small flapping specks circle the herbs and shrubs near you in warm sun (14–40 °C, never under closed crowns, never on snow). Set `profile` to `shinobi` for the ninja game (the extra keys are in the "Shinobi profile only" table below). **The wind you can see** (§DA, Wind I; `data/wind.json`): the weather's wind with gusts that travel. A gust is a patch of stronger air (up to 1.6 times the mean, lulls down to 0.45, turning the direction up to 10°) carried downwind at the wind's own speed, so you see it come: a paler, bowed patch runs over the grass toward you, then the trees round you toss. Every plant reads the same field at its own spot, so neighbours move together. Under trees everything below the crowns is sheltered by how much sky that spot sees (under a closed roof the understory gets 15 % of the wind) while the crowns take the full wind. What moves follows the real Beaufort signs (`wind.json crowns`): grass and herbs bow from 1.6 m/s (nearly flat by 13.9), leaves stir from 1.6, twigs from 3.4, small branches from 5.5, small trees sway from 8, big branches from 10.8, whole trees from 13.9; each trunk sways at its own period by its height. Aspens, poplars and the sacred fig tremble in any air (`wind.json flutter`). The shadow-dapple under trees shifts with the same gusts. **Wind II:** where leaves lie (the real litter, never conjured), a gust past 5.5 m/s at the ground (after the shelter of any crowns over it) lifts them: each is its tree's own leaf in its season's colour, tumbling end over end, skipping downwind and lying down again after 2–6 s, at most 40 at once round you. Conifer needles need 9 m/s. Sand streams over dunes, beaches and hot desert from 6.5 m/s, spindrift over snow from 5, dust off dry bare ground and roads in dry country from 7, as low scrolling streaks. Under a closed wood the gusts rarely reach the ground, so this happens on roads, clearings and edges. The bed's wind loop swells with the gust at you. The crowns round you (within 40 m) are sound sources when the gust there passes 1.6 m/s, at most four at once, each from its own tree: needles hush, broad leaves rustle, palm fronds clatter, dry autumn leaves rattle. Your cloak and every cloaked figure's take the gust where they stand, sheltered by the crowns over them. The fire's specks and the torch's sparks drift with the gust at the fire, and the coals flare as a gust reaches them. **Wind III:** water is a mirror under 1 m/s; past 2.5 m/s a gust's patch roughens as it slides over (its glints break up and the blue deepens a little); from 8 m/s a few pale pixel crests on lakes and the sea, never foam sheets. On part-cloudy days (§CX's cover between 0.15 and 0.85) the clouds' shadows sail over the land at 1.8 times the ground wind, patches 200–800 m across with hard edges: in one, the lit side goes to shade (navy or olive, as shade always is) and the sun's shadows stay where they are. Clear days have none and overcast is all shade already. A hearth under trees sends its smoke straight up through the crowns and leans only above them, the lean following the gusts at each height, and a gust at the fire tears the low column into rags.

The road follows the river to a people's camp, and a second road leaves the other way when there is a ruin to lead to. You hold only the torch: **the spear and the bow are no longer laid by you.** You find them at delve hearts (§CJ), or at a living camp with a maker, who leaves the one their people make (a spear, or a bow if they fletch) by the fire for you to take, once (§ED.7). **Rivers have phases** (`data/water/phases.json`): each stretch is read from its slope and width as a pool, glide, riffle, run, rapid, cascade or fall. A pool lies flat and dark, a glide shows slow streaks, a riffle breaks into small white flecks over stones, and a run, rapid or cascade carries more and more white water and foam lines. Foam lines and the odd drifting leaf move downstream with the current, and the water's sound swells and rises in pitch with the phase. Fish bite only in pools and glides. A spear thrown into a run or anything rougher is carried off downstream and lost (the log says so). **Every camp keeps a book** (`data/camp_books.json`): a book or a scroll on a low stand by the hearth (on the library's shelf once the camp reaches storage, §EN), with a quill and an ink pot. Right click it to read; it opens at the newest page, and Esc closes it. Its lines are the camp's own events (the hearth relit, a birth, the store, someone lost gathering, folk leaving for a fire), each stamped in game time, in ink that browns as it ages. | Left mouse past full charge | Super meter (the gold ring round the gauge, filled by perfect wall jumps, rolls and swing releases, and by hits): keep holding past full charge until the gauge fills again in red for a super shot (triple damage, faster, farther, a red streak); a super-thrown spear kills what it hits outright and pins it. At 25 m/s a spear thrust kills anything but a mythical creature; a thrust into a trunk or wall at speed hurts you instead |
| V or F5 | Switch between first and third person |

Gamepad:
- Left stick moves, and clicking it in (held) sprints.
- A jumps, B sneaks, X interacts, Y cycles what's in hand.
- The right trigger draws and shoots.
- Start opens the inventory, Back the map.
- Shinobi profile only: B in the air drops fast (and rolls on landing), A at a wall wall-jumps, X held clings and swings, and clicking the right stick switches first and third person.

Readouts: your speed at the bottom right (mph and km/h; faint when you're slow, brighter toward 120 km/h; in the shinobi profile its glow warms as the super meter fills), and an old railway pocket watch at the top right: a steel case and crown, a white dial with the hours 1-12 in black and 13-24 in red inside them, a minute track, black hands and a red seconds hand (the minute hand goes round once a game hour, 6 real minutes; the seconds hand once a game minute). These two are the only readouts pinned in a new game; the others (the time, the moon, its mansion, the season, the biome and soil, the temperature, the weather, the wind, your elevation and position) show with H, or all the time once you pin them (Esc, then click them). "Swimming" and "Above the clouds" show whenever they apply. All the on-screen text grows with the window.

The picture (design §ES, 3D pixel art; the frame by §EU.1): the game draws at a fixed 480 lines (854×480, the "default" pixel size) and blows that up to your window with square, unsmoothed pixels, text and all, so a bigger window only means bigger pixels. With integer scaling on (the default) it uses a whole multiple when the window holds at least two (1440p at exactly 3×; a 1080p screen at 2× with black bars round it, 4K at 4×); turn it off in settings to fill the screen. The other pixel sizes, 270 ("painted", §ES's old default) up to 720 lines, stay in settings. The HUD text and the watch scale with the pixel size, so they keep their share of the screen. The light is painted into the models (pre-lit): each surface is lit from the sky above, folds, the inside of a hood, the heart of a tree's crown and the foot of a trunk are darker toward navy (olive on green things), and the sun only tints and casts its shadow; firelight still rounds what it lights. `PRELIT=0` in the environment (or `look.json` `prelit.on` false) puts the old live lighting back to compare. Far trees past 200 m are small sprites of their own models.

Distance and shadows (design §ER.1): the world is drawn to the render distance (455 m by default) and the fog is full just inside it, lighter and bluer with distance; past it only the pale silhouettes of mountains show against the sky. The sun casts no shadow in a storm or at night (and the moon none); the two fires or torches nearest you cast soft shadows within 24 m, the rest light without them. Rain is one full-screen pass of streaks.

Health: the thin blue bar at the bottom left, with its number. It doesn't come back on its own; stand or sit still by a lit campfire to heal. Nothing hostile can hurt you by a lit fire. Falls hurt (more the farther you fall), and crashing into a trunk or wall at high speed hurts, and can kill.

Dying: your body stays where you fell, with everything you carried and wore (no marker; birds start circling over it after a while). You wake by the nearest campfire, carried there by the folk who found you, with nothing. Go back and right click your body to take it all back.

Carrying: ten things at most. Past six you're slower, climb slower and make more noise.

Climbing: walk up to a tree and right click it. W and S go up and down the trunk, and A and D go around it. At a fork, look out along a limb and push W to go onto it. Push toward another limb to reach across.

## 6. If something goes wrong
- **"This project was made with a different version":** you opened it with a Godot other than 4.3. Use 4.3 (step 1).
- **A black screen for more than about 20 seconds:** the planet is still generating, or your graphics card doesn't support the Forward+ renderer. Try updating your graphics drivers.
- **An F-key does nothing:** click inside the game window first. When the editor window has focus, F-keys go to the editor instead (there, F5 runs the game and F8 stops it).
