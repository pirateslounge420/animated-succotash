# How to open and play the project (no coding needed)

## 1. Get Godot 4.3
- The project is built for **Godot 4.3 (stable), the standard build** — not the ".NET" build, and not 4.4 or later.
- Download it from the Godot archive: https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.3-stable/
  - Windows: "Standard — 64-bit". You get a zip; unzip it anywhere. The program is `Godot_v4.3-stable_win64.exe`.
  - macOS: "Standard — Universal". Drag `Godot.app` into Applications. The first time, right-click it and choose Open.
  - Linux: "Standard — x86_64". Unzip; the program is `Godot_v4.3-stable_linux.x86_64`.
- Godot needs no installer and changes nothing else on your computer.
- (Claude Code's cloud machine has its own copy at `/root/bin/godot`, version 4.3.stable.official 77dcf97d8. That machine has no screen, so to play you need Godot on your own computer.)

## 2. Get the project
- On GitHub, open `pirateslounge420/animated-succotash` and switch the branch menu to `claude/lowpoly-exploration-prototype-i1z8g3`.
- Click **Code → Download ZIP**, and unzip it somewhere easy to find, such as Documents.
- (If you already use Git: `git clone` the repository and check out that branch.)

## 3. Open it in Godot
1. Start Godot. The **Project Manager** window opens.
2. Click **Import**, go into the unzipped folder, pick the file **`project.godot`**, and click **Import & Edit** (on some versions, **Open**).
3. The first time, Godot imports everything. Wait for the progress bar to finish (a minute or two).
   - The game no longer needs this import to look right (design §CG). The world's textures, the clouds, the species tiles and the HUD font are read straight from their files on disk, so the plants and the ground look the same whether or not the import has run or finished. Where an import is missing, the Output panel shows one warning naming the file. F3 in the game shows where the world's textures came from ("World textures: 9 from disk"). An exported build will need the raw PNGs included (see PROGRESS, 2 Oct §CG).
4. You're now in the editor. You don't need to touch anything in it.

## 4. Play
- Press **F5**, or click the **▶ Play** button at the top right. That runs the main scene, `scenes/main.tscn`.
- A game window opens. For a few seconds the planet is generated: the full one, 4,000 km around (a tenth of Earth's; mountains and seas at a tenth of their height). Then you wake by the campfire at the first camp.
- `data/dev.json` already sets this up:
  - `dev_mode: true` turns the F2–F9 dev keys on.
  - `postage_stamp: false` keeps play on the full planet. The "postage stamp" (a small test planet 40 km around with every kind of biome) is only for the checks and renders: they ask for it themselves with `STAMP=1` in the environment (for example `STAMP=1 godot --headless --path . --script tools/camp_check.gd`). Setting it to `true` here would make play use the stamp too; leave it off.
  - `seed: 42` and `spawn_choice: -1` are the dev tools' pins (the renders and checks set them themselves; -1 means "no pinned camp"). In play they apply only with `pin_in_play: true` (it is `false`) or `DEV_PIN=1` in the environment (design §CB). Otherwise **every new world rolls its own seed and its own first camp**, and booting continues the last world played (`user://worlds/last.json` points at it; old worlds stay by seed, nothing is deleted). The seed is the world's name: the log's first line reads "World 7731 — day 1". Every new world opens on **Day 1** in the afternoon; the day goes up at midnight where you stand, and the HUD's time, the clock face and the log's stamps all read the same clock (design §CG). For another world: **Settings (O) > World > New world** (it asks once; this world stays saved), or **F12** twice in dev mode.
  - `day_length_min: 144` is the real cycle: 144 minutes for a full day and night (6 real minutes an in-game hour; at the equator on an equinox: day 07:00–17:00, dusk 17:00–20:00, night 20:00–04:00, dawn 04:00–07:00). You wake in the afternoon, 22 real minutes before dusk (design §BX; `data/roads.json` `opening_road.spawn`). Lower it (say 20) to see the cycle go by faster.
- To stop, press **Esc** to free the mouse and close the game window. Or, back in the editor, press the **■ Stop** button.
- **Where plants grow** (design §CA, `data/habitat.json`): a species grows only in a biome whose file lists it (or, for a `data/plants` catalogue entry, in the biomes its own `biomes` list names); climate, soil and the realm still apply inside. `python3 tools/plant_schema_check.py --strict` requires the lists and prints each biome's species count and the thin spots; `python3 tools/biome_species_check.py SAVANNA` lists what the gate allows there.
- **The walkabout** (`tools/walkabout.gd`, the end-of-pass check in place of the dev frame): `SEED=101 xvfb-run -a -s "-screen 0 1280x720x24" godot --path . --rendering-method forward_plus --resolution 1280x720 -s tools/walkabout.gd` walks one seed's first camp, the first road and three random biomes in first person at three hours, saves the frames under `tools/reference/walkabout/<seed>/` and appends every species it found, with PASS/FAIL, to `tools/reference/walkabout/walkabout.txt`. `tools/reference/walkabout/run.sh` does the four seeds. `tools/no_import_check.sh` runs the game with the editor's import cache hidden (as on a machine that never imported); `tools/no_import_check.sh -- env QUICK=1 SITES=opening_camp xvfb-run … -s tools/walkabout.gd` does the walkabout that way, in a fresh random world. `tools/day_check.sh` checks Day 1 and the one clock on four seeds. `SITES=nests` adds the walkabout's nest sites (the nearest cave mouth, grotto, cenote, escarpment, waterfall or slot canyon, four kinds); `tools/nest_report.sh` counts the nests, the camps by people and the remains on four seeds; `SEED=7731 godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 --script tools/nest_check.gd` stands at a nest of each kind and checks what is built. `SITES=delve` walks the nearest barrow's delve: outside, at the stairhead, in the first room and the heart by torchlight at noon, and at the cairn. The same command line with `--script tools/old_hearth_check.gd` checks the cold hearths and the tomb lamps (`TOMB=barrow`, `graveyard` or `pyramid` picks the tomb); `tools/delve_check.gd` checks a barrow's delve (the pieces, every opened bit of ground roofed, floors and headroom, full dark at noon, the hearth, the find, the slab door); `tools/shift_check.gd` steps a world through dusk and dawn and checks who is out and the order the night's sounds come in. For §CN: `tools/swing_check.gd` (the swing lights a laid fire and an unlit torch, nothing else, and right click no longer lights), `tools/old_hearth_check.gd` (now also the kindling: not laid, wet, wet birch bark, dry, a flare, embers), `tools/overrun_check.gd` (the share, the sim, the holders, the heart's fire clearing it) and `tools/settle_check.gd` (folk come back to a cleared ruin, never to one still held). `SITES=delve` now walks the nearest overrun barrow and lights its heart's fire.

## 5. Keys (keyboard and mouse)
The game plays in the **ambient profile** (`data/movement.json` `profile`, design 30 Sept §AT–§BA): a slow first-person walk. Space jumps, right click interacts and climbs, and the wall jump, cling, bounce, swing, redirect, roll and super meter are off. You wake with nothing; a bundle of torches lies by every camp fire (right click takes one, Q brings it to hand). **The swing passes the flame** (design 2 Oct §CN): left click with the torch in hand swings it, and at the end of the swing the flame passes to whatever it touches, either way: an unlit torch swung through a lit fire or a planted torch catches; a lit torch lights a laid cold fire, embers or a planted torch gone out. It never lights the ground, grass, camps, folk or animals, and does nothing to an animal. Right click the ground with a lit torch to plant it, right click it to take it back. Fires burn down: right click fuel lying about (logs, branches, brush, dung, reeds, whatever the biome offers) to gather it, right click the fire with it to feed it. Embers come back with dry fuel. **A fire that is fully out needs kindling** (§CN): right click it with kindling to lay it, with at least one piece of fuel on it, then swing a lit torch through it; the flame takes in a moment. Kindling is whatever is fine and dry where you are: right click the ground at your feet for dead twigs, leaves, needles or dry grass (crouch first if you hold a lit torch, or the right click plants it), or right click the right plant (a birch for its bark). The pouch keeps what you carry dry (§CO): only kindling gathered while it rains, outside a roof, starts damp, and it dries in the pouch in six game hours. Damp, only birch bark, fatwood and Douglas-fir pitchwood will catch; the rest smoke. You can gather in any weather. Kindling with no fuel flares and goes out. Right click a camp's fire to make it your hearth: you wake there when you die. At night, away from a fire, the dark closes in: listen. Roads run between the ruins and camps (nobody keeps them: bridges out, trails that end at a collapse, waymarks, finds set just off the trail); rivers carry you downstream and won't let you swim up a fast reach; cloaked travellers walk the roads and never speak. Set `profile` to `shinobi` for the ninja game (the extra keys are in the "Shinobi profile only" table below).

**The camps are alive** (design 30 Sept §BL–§BT): each camp lives one of seventeen lives by its site (coast, marsh, river, old growth, steppe, desert, karst, canopy…; `data/peoples/`), and the log says who lives there the first time you come to its fire. By day the folk gather wood and food into a woodpile and a food store by the fire (no HUD: look at the pile), feed the fire from it, eat, have children who grow into teens and adults, and climb a ladder from fire to food to storage, when a headman and a plantkeeper appear, to a maker where the site allows. Right click the woodpile or the food store with fuel, food or seeds in hand to give it to them (seeds that take in that soil get planted: a plot by the fire). **Right click the headman** and he shows you his people's technique, once, for good: a line and hook (at the water's edge with a branch and dry grass or reeds in your pack, right click the water: you cut a pole and twist a line; then hold the pole and click at the water to cast, wait for the bite, click again to hook it), the coppice (with empty hands, right click a hazel, ash, willow or alder to cut it to the stool; it regrows poles on its own clock, right click the stool to take them), the resin torch (right click a conifer with an unlit torch in hand: it burns longer and shrugs off drizzle), the ember carrier (right click a lit fire with empty hands and no fuel in the pack to take a coal; right click the ground within a day to lay a new fire from it; left too long it goes cold), the fat lamp (knowing it, right click a camp's food store with empty hands to make one for a unit of their food; right click the ground to set it down: a dim light for hours that never blows out). The rest are flags for now. A camp whose fire is kept low for two nights is taken by the dark (blood by the fire, the survivors walk to the nearest lit fire); one that goes hungry for a week moves on; an empty camp is a ruin in two months and the forest takes it back in a year; its needful things stay by the dead fire, and feeding a camp's embers in time saves it. Ruins carry their old people's marks (a shell midden, weir stakes, house pits…) that a new camp clears and restores as it climbs. In a dry spell in fire-prone country, lightning or a lit torch dropped in the grass can start a wildfire: a scar the plants and the ground remember; a camp in its path walks away and rebuilds a valley over. **Nests** (design 1 Oct §CK, `data/landforms.json`, tier 1): people also live at the land's own places, and an emptied camp's remains lie there. Under a cliff's overhang or in a cave mouth (a rock roof over a first chamber, a dark low passage at the back), in a grotto (a low arch in wet limestone, a passage back to a drip pool), on a cenote's rim (a round shaft down to clear water, the way down a slope of fallen blocks), at the tail of a waterfall's pool, on a ravine's floor by its stream or spring, under an escarpment overhang (on open grass, a buffalo jump: cairn lanes on top narrowing to the lip), beside a glowing bay (warm lagoon water by mangrove that lights blue-green only where something stirs it, best at night). In dry sandstone the ravines pinch to slot canyons with sandy beds. Each nest is untouched, has a living camp, or holds an old camp's remains (that people's marks round the hearth); the people fit the place (shelter folk and karst folk live at the caves and sinkholes). Ferns grow at grottos, in a waterfall's spray and at a slot's seep. F3 lists the nearest nests. Somewhere in each world, once, an old sacred fig stands on a low rise in tropical dry forest (else jungle, else savanna), someone sitting beneath it, very still (§CL).

**Old hearths** (Mike, 2 Oct): wherever people once kept a fire and nobody does now (every ruin with no camp, every nest holding an old camp's remains), the hearth is still there: a ring of stones round cold ash and a couple of charred branches (they count as its fuel). Lay kindling in it (right click) and swing a lit torch through it to rekindle it, then feed it like any fire. Lit, it holds the dark off and you can make it your hearth. Untended, it burns down and goes out. Fire is still never made: the torch is the only way to light one. **Tomb lamps:** the stone lamps in a barrow, a desert pyramid, a mastaba or a graveyard's mausoleum stand dark until the ruin's hearth burns (its people's fire, or its old hearth rekindled), then come on, and go out when the fire dies. **Delves** (design 1 Oct §CJ, the first built): every barrow in stone or snow country leads down. Its end chamber is a stairhead, a stair goes down under the mound into full dark (no sun or moon reaches; the outdoor sounds give way to a drone and drips; the dark closes in as at night, whatever the hour, held back by your torch). Below: a first room with an old hearth you can rekindle (the one safe room) and bone niches or a fallen slab; another stair; the heart, where the dead lie with a spear or a bow you can take (once); and a stair up to a small cairn beyond the barrow, whose stone slab you push open from inside (right click; from outside it won't move). No traps or puzzle doors. **Overrun ruins** (§CN): about a third of the old delve barrows are held by the dark, and so is any camp the dark took that had a delve under it, once it has gone to ruin. By day the sounds go quiet near one and bones and scat lie at the barrow's door; at night a shape stands in the doorway, something calls, and near it the dark closes in faster. Below, whatever holds it waits in the dark rooms at any hour. Each room has a cold fire-holder (the first room's old hearth; the heart's ring of ash): laid and lit, each holds its room, and nothing comes within its light while it burns (three pieces of fuel at most). Light the heart's and the ruin is cleared: they leave by the way out and the log says so. The hearth up top can be lit any time as a safe base, but it doesn't clear the ruin. A cave mouth or grotto the dark took is the one exception (§CO): its den is only the small chamber behind the mouth, so the hearth at its opening, laid and lit, clears it. Keep the cleared ruin's hearth burning and about twelve game hours later folk come to it (its survivors, if it fell lately, else a few from a full camp within 40 km): it is a living camp again, a hearth you can take. **Dusk** (design 1 Oct §CH): day animals go to bed as the light goes (each at its own moment, walking off), night ones come out of sight, and the sound follows the sun: the cicadas of the day fade, then the first night insects (different in each kind of country), then the frogs where there is water; dawn backwards.
In really old growth the **canopy folk** live up in the giants on platforms joined by vine bridges, their fire in a clay box; no ladder comes down for a stranger: climb the trunk (right click it) past the deck and let go. Once the headman has met you their rope ladder hangs from the hearth deck: right click its foot to climb it, its top to come down.

You play in first person (the ambient profile has no third-person view). Walk and sprint speeds, the jump and every other movement and weapon number are in `data/movement.json` and `data/combat.json` (each part explained at the top of the file); edit them and restart the game.

Gamepad:
- Left stick moves, and clicking it in (held) sprints.
- A jumps, B sneaks, X interacts, Y cycles what's in hand.
- The right trigger draws and shoots.
- Start opens the inventory, Back the map.
- Shinobi profile only: B in the air drops fast (and rolls on landing), A at a wall wall-jumps, X held clings and swings, and clicking the right stick switches first and third person.

Readouts: your speed at the bottom right (mph and km/h; faint when you're slow, brighter toward 120 km/h; in the shinobi profile its glow warms as the super meter fills), and an old railway pocket watch at the top right: a steel case and crown, a white dial with the hours 1-12 in black and 13-24 in red inside them, a minute track, black hands and a red seconds hand (the minute hand goes round once a game hour, 6 real minutes; the seconds hand once a game minute). These two are the only readouts pinned in a new game; the others (the time, the moon, its mansion, the season, the biome and soil, the temperature, the weather, the wind, your elevation and position) show with H, or all the time once you pin them (Esc, then click them). "Swimming" and "Above the clouds" show whenever they apply. All the on-screen text grows with the window.

The picture: the game draws at a fixed 480 lines (854×480) and blows that up to your window with square, unsmoothed pixels, text and all, so a bigger window only means bigger pixels. With integer scaling on (the default) it uses a whole multiple when the window holds at least two (a 1080p screen shows it at exactly 2×, with thin black bars round it); turn it off in settings to fill the screen at 2.25×. 720 lines is the most the settings allow.

Health: the thin blue bar at the bottom left, with its number. It doesn't come back on its own; stand or sit still by a lit campfire to heal. Nothing hostile can hurt you by a lit fire. Falls hurt (more the farther you fall), and crashing into a trunk or wall at high speed hurts, and can kill.

Dying: your body stays where you fell, with everything you carried and wore (no marker; birds start circling over it after a while). You wake by the nearest campfire, carried there by the folk who found you, with nothing. Go back and right click your body to take it all back.

Carrying: ten things at most. Past six you're slower, climb slower and make more noise.

Climbing: walk up to a tree and right click it. W and S go up and down the trunk, and A and D go around it. At a fork, look out along a limb and push W to go onto it. Push toward another limb to reach across.

## 6. If something goes wrong
- **"This project was made with a different version":** you opened it with a Godot other than 4.3. Use 4.3 (step 1).
- **A black screen for more than about 20 seconds:** the planet is still generating, or your graphics card doesn't support the Forward+ renderer. Try updating your graphics drivers.
- **An F-key does nothing:** click inside the game window first. When the editor window has focus, F-keys go to the editor instead (there, F5 runs the game and F8 stops it).
